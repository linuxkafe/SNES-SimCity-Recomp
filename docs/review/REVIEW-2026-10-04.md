## The BLOCKER, SUSTAINED — and it is the rubric's own criterion

**Filed by Pragmatico (E-03, and a structural gap in the guard).**

`scripts/clock-gate.sh:720` and `README.md:222`/`:903` assert:

> *"the reference build was MEASURED at 29 of them"*

**This claim is retracted.** T110 established that the reference's headless renders
are **tile data, not a game screen**, and that "29 distinct date images" is a count of
**WRAM pairs** (`$0B53`, `$0B55`) — not 29 screen images. The peer cannot supply a
picture-based positive control at all.

**Rubric E-03 reads, verbatim:**

> *"No retracted claim survives as an assertion in the clock-gate's failure text —
> `scripts/clock-gate.sh` FAIL message contains no claim that appears in the
> retraction log"*

**So this is a direct E-03 violation, and it is a BLOCKER** under the rubric's
definition: a retracted claim presented as established fact, in the text of the
project's central gate.

**And here is the part that matters more than the finding itself.**
`scripts/retracted-claims.tsv` has **no row whose pattern matches this phrase**
(`grep -c "MEASURED at 29" scripts/retracted-claims.tsv` → **0**). Therefore
`check-retracted-claims.sh` — and `make check-claims` — report **PASS, 0 violations**
while the false assertion sits in the most-read document and in the gate's own
failure output.

**The E-03 criterion has an executable check, and that check is vacuous.** It asks
whether a retracted claim appears in the gate text, but the ledger is **pattern-based
and only as good as the phrases someone happened to record**. A retracted claim with
no matching row is invisible to it. This is the same class of defect as
**CONF-20** — a guard that proves nothing about what it claims to police — and the
same lesson as the clock-gate's own detector, which was a brightness meter while
claiming to be a date detector.

**Two closure conditions, both required:**
1. the phrase is removed from `clock-gate.sh` and `README.md`, or restated as what it
   actually is (a WRAM pair count, with the screen-level control **not established**);
2. the E-03 check gains the ability to notice a retracted claim that has **no** ledger
   row — or E-03 is downgraded in the next rubric from a checkable criterion to an
   aspiration, **in writing**.

**NOT ACTIONED in this round**, deliberately: it is gate failure text, and the
project's rule is that gate text is not edited to obtain a result. Removing an
unfounded assertion is the *opposite* of loosening a gate, but the change is
escalated rather than made quietly by the moderator.

## The methodological finding underneath all of it

Three of this round's findings were **the guard being wrong, not the code**:

- a date detector that was a **brightness meter**;
- an E-03 check that is **vacuous** against any retracted claim not already
  pattern-matched in the ledger;
- a self-test with **no positive control** on the real corpus.

**None of the three would ever have been found by adding more tests to the code.** They
were found by asking, of each guard, *"what would this report if it were wrong in a way
nobody has thought of?"* — which is a reviewer question, not a testing question.

## What would move this to APPROVE

Close the 7 MAJORs, beginning with the E-03 violation and its vacuous check; register a
new rubric whose checks are executable (C-04 via `git archive`, I-02 via a pipe, neither
writing to `/tmp`) and which covers rendered copyrighted imagery; add a corpus
positive-control to `check-entrypoints`; and obtain one genuinely independent human
reviewer — four single-model personas are the protocol's fallback, not independence.
