## Moderator reclassification — two BLOCKERs downgraded, with reasons

The personas filed two BLOCKERs. I checked both myself and **do not sustain
either as a BLOCKER.** Recording the disagreement rather than silently accepting
the personas' severity is the point of a moderator.

### Cinico's BLOCKER, G-02 "FAIL message asserts an unproven cause" → **MAJOR**

The claim is half right, and the half that is right is not the half alleged.
`scripts/clock-gate.sh` opens its FAIL text with:

> *"The city not simulating is the established fact. WHY it does not simulate is
> NOT ESTABLISHED, and this gate deliberately does not guess."*

and then explicitly warns that a **previous** version of the message named a
cause and that the cause was retracted. **That is exemplary and is the opposite
of the allegation.**

The real defect is in the **final** paragraph: it states *"So the two builds are
separated AT THE INSTRUCTION, not at the symptom"* — an **inference presented as
fact** — and then proposes as *"Next measurement: count executions of `$03:8026`
over f3000-f13080"* something **already measured** by T102 (14 000 frames, zero
executions, C-041 confirmed, on both tiers and two machines). Pragmatico and
Utilizador independently hit the same paragraph from different angles.

**Why MAJOR and not BLOCKER:** the rubric reserves BLOCKER for a **published
claim that is false**. Nothing here is shown false; it is **stale and
over-claiming**. The gate's headline refuses to name a cause, and its verdict is
FAIL either way. Independently confirmed as a real defect: Utilizador.

**Closure:** the final paragraph is rewritten to match what is now established —
`$03:8026` is measured zero over 14 000 frames (T102/C-041), the "next
measurement" is removed, and any causal statement is labelled as inference.

### Utilizador's BLOCKER, I-01 "301 KB rendered-game-frame bitmap is tracked" → **MAJOR, and the rubric has a gap**

**Why not BLOCKER under I-01:** I-01 reads *"The ROM is never committed"*

**So the finding stands as MAJOR and the rubric is the thing that is defective:**

Registering a new rubric is the fix; **this one must not be edited** — it is
pre-registered and hashed.

**Closure:** the four PNGs are removed from tracking (or moved out of the repo and
linked from a location the README documents as user-supplied), and a new rubric
is registered with an I-criterion covering *rendered copyrighted imagery*, not
just the ROM file.

## Convergence is not corroboration — one finding examined and reframed

**All three personas independently filed "README's 186 AOT symbols is stale /
does not reproduce."** Three-for-three looks like strong signal, and this project
has a documented history of exactly that trap, so it was checked rather than
counted.

`README.md:154-158` is a table with **two different cells**:

| routines in the manifest | 302 |
| recompiled ahead of time (`aot_eligible`) | **239** |
| distinct AOT symbols emitted into `src/gen/*.c` | **186** |

**The personas compared 186 against 239/241 — two different rows of the same
table.** 239 is the count of eligible routines; 186 is the count of distinct
symbols *emitted into the generated C*. They are not supposed to be equal.

My own re-derivation attempts did not settle it either: a naive grep of all
function signatures in `src/gen/*.c` yields **559**, a third quantity again.

**So the finding is reframed, and the reframed version is the real one:**

> **The `186` is neither confirmed nor refuted — it is not re-derivable from the
> repository, because the method that produced it was never written down.**
> (E-04; reproducibility.)

That is a stronger and more actionable finding than "the number is wrong", and it
is **Purista's** lens. **Purista produced nothing** (below), and this is
precisely the class of finding it existed to make.

## Process defect: the rubric's own verifiable checks cannot be executed

**Purista filed no findings. It died on a permission wall, and the wall was the
rubric's own command.**

`RUBRIC.md` I-02's verifiable check is:

    git ls-files study/ | grep -vE "..." > /tmp/out.txt; wc -l /tmp/out.txt

The redirects into `/tmp` are **auto-rejected** by the opencode permission system
as `external_directory`. Purista could not run the criterion, and — correctly —
**did not fabricate a result.** It stopped.

**The same class of defect killed the first attempt at this entire round.** Three
of four reviewers terminated because C-04's check is:

    cp -r . /tmp/relocated && cd /tmp/relocated && make build && make test

which is (a) **auto-rejected** on `/tmp/*`, and (b) **impractical**: the working
tree is **1.7 GB**, of which **1.6 GB is six untracked build directories**
(`build/`, `build-dbg/`, `build-instr/`, `build-instr-tr/`, `build-prof/`,
`build-tr/`, `build-rel/`). `git archive HEAD` is **3 368 960 bytes**.

**And `cp -r .` is not a clean checkout anyway** — it copies 1.6 GB of stale
build artefacts into the "relocated" tree, so running C-04 as literally written
would not test what the criterion claims to test. Pragmatico, Cinico and Utilizador
each found this independently from three different angles.

**This is a finding about the rubric, discovered by the rubric failing, and it is
the most transferable result of the round:** *a pre-registered criterion whose
verifiable check cannot be executed is not a criterion.* Both defects are in the
checks' **commands**, not their intent, so the fix is a new registered rubric with
executable checks — `git archive` for C-04, a pipe for I-02, neither writing to
`/tmp` — and **this rubric stays untouched**.

## Why not APPROVE, and what would change it

- **0 confirmed BLOCKERs, 14 open MAJORs.** The rubric's verdict table gives
  REJECT WITH CONDITIONS. It does not permit APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS while any
  MAJOR is open.
- **No independent human reviewer has ever reviewed this project.** Four
  single-model personas are the fallback the protocol specifies when
  independence is unavailable; they are **not** independence. All four ran the
  same model family via `freellmapi/auto`.
- **Self-authorship.** The author ran the convening, wrote the brief, and
  moderated. Protocol §4 makes the author ineligible.
- **The rubric has no executor.** Its criteria have never been machine-run, and
  two of its checks cannot be executed at all.

**What would move this to APPROVE:** close the 14 MAJORs; register a new rubric
whose checks are executable and which covers rendered copyrighted imagery;
establish one genuinely independent reviewer; and settle the crop detector's
INIDISP sensitivity.
