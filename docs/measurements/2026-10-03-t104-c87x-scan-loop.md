# Measurement — frame-resolve the `$03C87x` loop (T104, 2026-10-03)

**The question.** 86% of bank `$03`'s interpreted cost in f3000–f3271 sits in
four PCs around a loop at `$03C877`. C-069 named its shape from ROM bytes
(`STA $7F6B00,X` / `INX` / `CPX #$F4`), reported `$03C87F` as *not an instruction
boundary* because linear decode makes it the second byte of `8D D0 F6`, and left
three things OPEN: **the loop's last execution, its back-edge, and what it
scans.**

**The answers, all Deck-native.**

1. **Last execution: f3270.** The loop runs in **f3259–f3270 and nowhere else**,
   and the bank's own last frame is **f3271** (16 steps, a 16-instruction
   epilogue ending `RTL` at `$03:D2B7`). **F3 did not fire: C-039b's boundary is
   NOT wrong.** The loop dies one frame *before* the bank does, which is a
   different and smaller fact than the one the ticket was written to catch.
2. **Back-edge: `$03C87F`, and it is a real instruction.** The opcode byte
   *fetched* at `$03C87F` is `$D0`. Taken **36 339** times, not taken **once**.
3. **What it scans: nothing. It is a zero-fill, and it runs away.** `A = $0000`,
   one `$00` byte stored per iteration at `$7F6B00 + X`, with the full 16-bit X
   added (the `$9F` long-indexed form). X was **measured** walking
   `$0000` → `$04FF` (writing `$7F6B00`–`$7F6FFF`, 1 280 distinct addresses) and
   then on to `$14FF` (writing `$7F7000`–`$7F7FFF`), spilling across the
   f3259 → f3260 frame boundary. **It passes `X == $00F4` — its own bound —
   2 069 measured times and keeps going.**

**And the finding that changes the register: `$03C87E` never executes, and
`$03C87F` is the loop's only branch.** C-069's "not an instruction boundary"
clause is **refuted** as a statement about the executed stream (ledger
**R-038**). Its *cost* numbers are unaffected and reproduce to the unit.

All figures below are from the Steam Deck (`ssh deck@steamdeck`), trace tier
`build-tr`, `scripts/d_city.script`, headless. **Host-only: none.**

## 4. The back-edge — and R-038

ROM, LoROM rule `bank*0x8000 + (addr & 0x7FFF)`, file offsets shown:

```
$C86E  8D 4D 0B        STA $0B4D
$C871  A9 00 00        LDA #$0000          <- once
$C874  A2 00 00        LDX #$0000          <- once
$C877  9F 00 6B 7F     STA $7F6B00,X   ┐
$C87B  E8              INX             │ the loop, 4 instructions
$C87C  E0 F4           CPX #$F4        │
$C87E  8D D0 F6        (STA $F6D0)     ┘  <- linear decode's view; NEVER EXECUTES
$C87F  D0 F6           BNE $C877          <- the actual back-edge
$C881  20 77 B4        JSR $B477
```

**`CYC_WATCH` prints the opcode byte actually fetched.** That is the ground
truth C-069 did not have, and it is what makes this section a measurement rather
than a disassembly:

```
$03C877 op=$9F   36340
$03C87B op=$E8   36340
$03C87C op=$E0   36340
$03C87F op=$D0   36340
```

| measured | value |
|---|---|
| `$03C87E` in 2 676 196 `[itb]` lines | **0** |
| `$03C87E` in the whole-run bank-`$03` dump | **absent** |
| `$03C87F` successor `$03C877` (taken) | **36 339** |
| `$03C87F` successor `$03C881` (not taken) | **1**, at **f3270** |
| `$03C87C` successor | `$03C87F` ×36 340 — **`$03C87E` never, not once** |

The taken/not-taken split is exactly `D0 F6` = `BNE $C877`: `next($C87F)` is
`$C881`, `$881 − 10 = $C877`. Both arms land where `BNE` says they should.

> ### R-038 — C-069's `$03C87F` clause is retracted
>
> C-069 read `$03C87F` as "**not an instruction boundary** — the second byte of
> `8D D0 F6`" and, correctly applying C-056's rule to that *ROM-decode* fact,
> reported it "unattributed, not as an executed instruction". **The rule was
> applied faithfully and the conclusion is still wrong**, because it answers a
> question about the ROM where the question was about the **executed stream**.
> In the stream, `$03C87F` fetches `$D0`, is the loop's **only** branch, and is
> its back-edge 36 339 times. What is not an instruction boundary is
> **`$03C87E`** — and `$03C87E` never executes at all.
>
> This is C-056's rule failing in a new direction, and it is the rule's third
> appearance in this register (`$03:D947`/`$03D94B`, R-034's "next-PC does not
> match its length", and now this). **The generalisation: a byte-boundary
> question about the ROM cannot be answered by, or exported into, a claim about
> execution.** Where the two disagree, the fetched opcode byte settles it.
>
> **C-069's cost numbers are NOT retracted.** `$03C87C` 36 344, `$03C877` 36 344,
> `$03C87B` 36 343, `$03C87F` 36 340 all reproduce exactly (R1's trace and R3's
> dump). Its "86%" is unaffected.

## 5. Entry, exit, and what is *not* in the loop

Transitions, over the full 2 676 196-line stream (not a filtered one — an
earlier pass over a `$03C87x`-only filter produced four phantom "re-entries" that
were frame boundaries, and they are named here because that filter was wrong):

**5 entries into `$03C877`:**

| frame | from | |
|---|---|---|
| f3259 | `$03C874` `LDX #$0000` | the fall-through, after `$03C871` `LDA #$0000` |
| f3261 | **`$0081A3`** | `RTI` resume |
| f3266 | `$0081A3` | `RTI` resume |
| f3267 | `$0081A3` | `RTI` resume |
| f3268 | `$0081A3` | `RTI` resume |

**1 exit, ever:** f3270, `$03C87F → $03C881`. `$03C881` then runs **once in the
whole 14 000-frame run** and never again.

The four `RTI` resumes are the four interrupts taken *out of* the loop —
successors `$03C877/$03C87B/$03C87C → $0080B2`, 4/3/4 times. `$0081A3` is the
pushed return address. This is C-050 (bank `$03` is resumed by `RTI`) showing up
inside the loop, and it is why `$03C87B` reads one fewer than `$03C877`.

**Nothing runs between the test and the branch.** All **36 344** successors of
`$03C87C` are `[itb]` lines and **0** are `[aotblk]`; between `$03C87F` and
`$03C877`, **0** `[aotblk]`. So this is not an AOT block clobbering the flags
between the compare and the branch — that hypothesis is **measured out**, not
assumed away.

## 6. What it "scans" — it doesn't; it zero-fills, and it runs away

`WLOG_ADDR` + `WLOG_STATE` logs every write in a 16-bit range with frame, value,
width, `A`, `X`, and `IPC`. It funnels through `cpu_write8/16`, so it sees both
engines. Four windows, each its own run:

| window | stores by `IPC=$03C877` | X range | frames |
|---|---|---|---|
| `$6B00`–`$6BFF` (R4) | **511** | `$0000`–`$00FF`, each twice | f3259 |
| `$6B00`–`$6FFF` (R7) | **2 559** (1 280 distinct addrs) | `$0000`–`$04FF` | f3259 |
| `$7000`–`$7FFF` (R8) | **8 192** | `$04FF`–`$14FF` | **f3259 + f3260** |
| `$0000`–`$0FFF` (R9) | **0** | — | — |

Every one of those stores: `value = 00`, `width = w1`, **`A = 0000`**, and
`addr = $6B00 + X` for every row.

> **It is not a scan loop. `A = $0000` and it stores one zero byte per
> iteration at `$7F6B00 + X` — it is a memory clear, and the clear does not stop
> where the code says it stops.**

**It blows through its own bound.** From R7's 2 559 rows:

| | stores |
|---|---|
| `X ≤ $00F4` | **490** |
| **`X > $00F4`** | **2 069** |

The only test in the loop is `CPX #$F4` and the only exit is the `BNE` that
follows it, with nothing executing between them (§5). **The loop therefore took
its "continue" branch more than two thousand times while X was past the value it
was comparing against.** `LDX #$0000` runs **once**, and `INX` is the only
writer of X inside the loop, so X climbs monotonically — it cannot skip `$00F4`
by jumping over it.

The `$9F` form adds the **full 16-bit X** regardless of the X flag, which is
why `STA $7F6B00,X` reaches `$7F6FFF` and beyond while `WLOG_STATE` reports
`Xf=0`. That is correct 65816 behaviour and it is recorded so the next reader
does not "fix" it.

**Why it stops at f3270 is OPEN and no cause is claimed.** It is a runaway fill
that something else ends one frame before the gate closes. Whether the fill is
*supposed* to run this long is not established either — this build's `$0014`
bit 7 and the reference's behaviour at this address are both unexamined.

## 8. My own instrument failed twice, and both are recorded

The discipline this project runs on is only worth anything if the failures are
published with the successes.

1. **A relative `--script` path.** The first launch of all three runs died in
   four seconds with `script: cannot open 'scripts/d_city.script'` /
   `script: the host chdir()s to the exe dir; pass an absolute path`. The runbook
   says this; I did it anyway. Eleven runs' worth of launch was lost to it, and
   the fix is `"$HOME/simcity/scripts/d_city.script"`.
2. **A filtered stream manufactured four phantom re-entries.** Adjacency tested
   over `$03C87x` lines only produced `C877 -> C877`, which read as the loop
   restarting. They were frame boundaries — other banks' code runs between
   iterations across frames. Re-run over the **full** 2 676 196-line stream,
   there are 5 entries and 1 exit. **A filter that removes the thing that
   explains the artefact will always invent the artefact.**
3. **My own 65816 length table was wrong** and I checked it before believing it.
   The first pass labelled `$D0` (BNE rel8) as length 1 and flagged **460**
   sites. That is my table's error rate, not the project's, and none of those
   460 is reported as a finding. The four loop PCs are exempt from this because
   `CYC_WATCH` supplied their **fetched** opcode bytes.

## 9. What this does NOT establish

- **Not a cause for the freeze.** The loop is a runaway memory clear that ends
  one frame before bank `$03` dies. Nothing here says why the bank dies, why the
  clear runs, or why the city does not simulate. **C-006 stays OPEN.**
- **Not why `$03:C87F` is where it is.** Two measured anomalies — `$03C87E`
  skipped, and `CPX`/`BNE` not honouring `X == $00F4` — are consistent with a
  single defect in how `$E0` (`CPX #imm`) is decoded: a length one byte too long
  explains the skip, and a comparison that never sets Z explains the runaway.
  **That is a hypothesis with two predictions, both met. It is not a
  measurement, the flags were never read, and other mechanisms are not
  excluded. No cause is claimed.**
- **Not the loop's extent.** X was followed to `$14FF`. Above that, nothing was
  watched; the loop ran 36 340 iterations, so most of its stores were never
  observed.
- **Not whether the `$7F6B00` page matters.** 1 280+ addresses were written and
  not examined.
- **Step counts are instrumented-build figures** (C-048/C-049). The *structural*
  results — 0 at `$03C87E`, 0 after f3270, 1 exit, 5 entries, 0 bank-`$03` AOT
  in f3272–4200 — are zero-vs-nonzero and do not depend on them. Note R1's trace
  reads `$03C877` = **36 344** and R3's counter reads **36 340** for the same PC
  in the same 3 300 vs 14 000-frame configuration; that 4-step spread is C-049
  again and is why no *count* is load-bearing here.
- **No peer source was touched.** `study/peer-linux/` was not read or used.

## 10. The single next measurement

**Read the flags at `$03C87F`.** One instrument change, one run: print the
processor status at the two loop edges, or single-step `$E0` in isolation to see
whether it sets Z for `X == $F4` and what length it advances the PC by. The
decode question and the runaway question are then one measurement, and the
hypothesis in §9 either becomes a cause or dies. **Until that is measured, both
anomalies stay OPEN.**
